Romantic Fashion Is Back: The SS27 Version Is Sharper Than Coquette

Lace has left the costume box. Copenhagen’s strongest SS27 looks pair romance with leather, long shorts, strange proportions and a sharper shoe.

by.foro Editorial10 August 202612 min read

The new romance

The romantic fashion trend for 2027 is lace without the sugar rush. Eyelet blouses, sheer dresses and delicate trims are being worn with leather jackets, tailored Bermuda shorts, capri leggings and shoes that look slightly too severe. The result is feminine, but it is not trying to look innocent.

Copenhagen Fashion Week’s SS27 street style made that shift especially visible. During the 3 to 7 August 2026 shows, editors and guests kept choosing romantic pieces, then interrupting them: a lace top above long shorts, a pale dress under black leather, a fine fabric grounded by a pointed mule. The tension is the outfit.

The concise answer

Romantic fashion now needs one resistant element

Wear one genuinely delicate piece, such as an eyelet blouse, lace skirt or translucent dress, then add something dry, practical or angular. Think leather, poplin, dark wool, Bermuda shorts, capris, a broad shoulder or a pointed shoe. Skip the pile-up of bows, pearls and pastel accessories. One romantic note reads as personal; six can read as styling shorthand.

Romance has moved on from the coquette uniform

The coquette boom gave fashion an instantly recognisable vocabulary: satin bows, ballet flats, tiny cardigans, rosettes, pearls and a very specific shade of pink. It was effective online because every prop reinforced the same message. It also became easy to imitate without much attention to cut, fabric or proportion.

The SS27 version is harder to reduce to a shopping list. Copenhagen street style coverage from Who What Wear and Marie Claire identified lace and eyelet alongside leather jackets, Bermuda shorts, capri leggings, pointed mules, kitten-heeled flip-flops, turquoise and mustard yellow. Those items do not belong to one neat aesthetic family. Their friction is what makes the current look feel alive.

This is a trend report, not a claim that everybody in Copenhagen dressed alike. Street style images are selected by photographers and editors, so they magnify some ideas and leave others out. Yet repeated combinations can still show how fashion’s grammar is changing. Lace is no longer automatically paired with another soft sign. It is being treated as texture.

The useful distinction: romantic is not girlish

Romantic clothing can be aged, stern, sensual, odd or practical. A Victorian blouse has romance because of its construction and historical echo, not because it makes the wearer look younger. A dark lace dress can be romantic without being sweet. Separating romance from girlhood opens the category to stronger colour, broader proportions and clothes that do not depend on coyness.

That is also why this movement connects naturally to literary chic without the costume. Both work best when the reference is felt through material and silhouette rather than displayed as a collection of props.

Lace has backbone when the cloth around it is plain

The eyelet blouse was one of the clearest Copenhagen signals. Its appeal is practical as well as visual. It replaces a tank or standard white shirt without changing the basic function of an outfit. You can still wear it with black trousers and sandals, but the punctured fabric introduces shadow, irregularity and a sense of age.

Look for eyelet with enough density to hold its own shape. Very thin cotton can collapse around the shoulders and make elaborate sleeves feel limp. A crisp cotton lawn or poplin keeps the holes legible and allows a cuff, collar or gathered shoulder to stand away from the body. Cream and optic white behave differently: cream looks older and quieter, while clear white creates sharper contrast with black or indigo.

How to avoid the costume problem

Do not repeat the blouse’s historical cues everywhere else. If it has a high neck, gathered sleeves and a scalloped hem, leave the hair simple and the jewellery spare. Wear it with a belt that looks contemporary, a plain bag and a bottom with a decisive line. A long black short is better than a floral skirt if the blouse already does the storytelling.

Sheer lace needs a considered base layer. Match the underlayer to the effect you want, not automatically to your skin. A black camisole under cream lace makes the pattern graphic. A same-colour bralette lowers contrast and keeps the surface quieter. A white bra under white eyelet often looks accidental because it highlights seams rather than the textile.

The still life above shows the palette in its most useful form. The lace is the fragile element. Charcoal wool makes it feel substantial, cobalt adds a clean interruption, mustard prevents the whole story becoming bridal, and a smooth silver cuff brings hard light into the mix. Nothing needs a bow.

The new proportion is deliberately awkward

Romantic dressing traditionally relies on a long, continuous line: full skirt, narrow waist, delicate shoe. SS27 street style keeps breaking that line with lengths that once felt difficult. Bermuda shorts end near the knee. Capri leggings stop at the calf. Pencil skirts narrow the stride. Each one makes the body negotiate the outfit rather than float through it.

With a decorative blouse, tailored Bermuda shorts are the easiest entry. Choose a pair with a proper waistband, pressed front or clean pleat, and enough room through the thigh to avoid looking like office trousers cut off in an emergency. The hem should sit where the leg begins to narrow. If it cuts across the widest part of the knee, adjust by two or three centimetres before abandoning the idea.

Capris are more particular. Their current appeal comes from treating them as a lean underlayer, not a substitute for full-length leggings. They work under a loose eyelet tunic, a sheer midi dress or a boxy leather jacket because the shortened line leaves room for the shoe. Dense matte jersey looks more intentional than shiny gym fabric.

Use volume once

If the blouse has a balloon sleeve, keep the short relatively straight. If the skirt is full, use a closer top and a jacket with a clear shoulder. Repeating volume at every level can make the outfit look as if it belongs to a period drama wardrobe department. One generous shape is enough to create movement.

The same discipline improves European summer dressing. Raffia, stripes and soft trousers become more convincing when one line stays exact. Romance needs that exact line even more because the fabric already carries emotion.

The wrong shoe makes the outfit current

The “wrong shoe” idea survives because a dress and shoe from the same mood can feel finished too soon. Change the shoe and the eye has to look again.

The shoe should differ in attitude but agree in weight. A kitten-heeled flip-flop works with a lace midi because both are visually light. A pointed mule answers the clean line of Bermuda shorts, while a worn loafer grounds a sheer skirt, particularly with a plain charcoal sock.

Heavy footwear is harder. If you want a substantial sole, repeat its density in a leather jacket, broad belt or sturdy bag so the shoe has an ally.

Comfort still decides the purchase

On a kitten-heeled flip-flop, check that the toe post does not pull and the upper holds the foot without making the toes grip. A pointed mule is often kinder because the vamp holds more of the foot. A softly squared point gives direction without compressing the toes.

Romantic colour is no longer limited to blush

The most interesting SS27 colour story is not a new pastel. Copenhagen coverage repeatedly noticed turquoise, while mustard yellow, cobalt and dark red appeared as confident accents. These colours stop lace from defaulting to wedding language.

Turquoise is particularly useful with tobacco brown, cream and black. It can arrive as one piece of jewellery, a small bag or a fine knit tied at the waist. Cobalt makes white eyelet feel almost graphic. Mustard warms ivory and dark denim but needs clean surroundings; combine it with too many browns and the outfit can become nostalgic.

Dark red is the quietest option. A burgundy mule or oxblood bag supplies romance through colour without adding decoration. It also connects beautifully with black leather, ecru cotton and grey wool. If your wardrobe is mostly neutral, this is the most durable accent to buy.

Keep the colour in one zone

For the strongest result, place saturated colour either near the face or at the edge of the outfit, not everywhere. A cobalt cardigan over cream lace draws the eye upward. A red shoe creates punctuation at the ground. Matching the shoe, bag and scarf in the same bright colour is technically coordinated but removes the tension that made the colour interesting.

1. Eyelet blouse and long black short

Tuck only the front enough to show the waistband and keep the bag structured. Every piece can separate into an ordinary wardrobe.

2. Lace dress and leather jacket

Let the jacket finish above the waist or below the fullest part of the hip, and wear it open so the dress remains one column.

3. Lace skirt and men’s T-shirt

Choose a firm neck and dry, weighty cotton. A loafer makes the skirt useful for daytime, while a sock creates a deliberate ankle break.

4. Tunic, capris and a small heel

Keep the tunic opaque through the body and concentrate embroidery at the sleeve or hem. The small heel moves the capri away from gym wear.

5. Slip skirt, grey knit and one brooch

Pin the brooch off-centre at the shoulder or collarbone. Our guide to wearing brooches in 2026 explains how placement changes its formality.

Buy for fabric and line, not the trend label

Turn an eyelet blouse inside out. Check whether the embroidered holes are clean, loose threads cross the openings or side seams pucker. On lace, look for motifs that meet reasonably at major seams. A flower cut awkwardly at the centre front will remain noticeable.

Check the care label before falling in love. A hand-wash-only dress may suit occasions but is a poor daily layer if it will wait indefinitely for special treatment. Buy the blouse if it improves five trousers you own, or the dark red shoe if it works with plain dresses and jeans. A worthwhile trend purchase creates combinations rather than demanding its own supporting cast.

What is likely to last beyond SS27

Fact: eyelet blouses, lace dresses, leather jackets, long shorts and pointed shoes were repeatedly visible in Copenhagen Fashion Week street style coverage in August 2026. Observation: editors styled them through contrast rather than a single coquette formula. Prediction: the lace blouse and dark red accessory have the strongest chance of lasting because they integrate easily into existing wardrobes.

Capri leggings may remain more seasonal. They create a recognisable silhouette and therefore risk feeling dated faster, but that does not make them a bad purchase if the proportion genuinely suits your clothes. The kitten-heeled flip-flop is likely to continue as an occasion shoe, though comfort will decide whether individuals keep wearing it.

The broader shift should outlast any one item. Romantic fashion has recovered texture without requiring innocence. It can now sit beside tailoring, utility and slightly severe accessories. That makes it less of an aesthetic identity and more of a useful styling language.

Questions about the romantic fashion trend

Is coquette fashion still in style for 2027?

Bows and ballet references have not vanished, but the newer romantic direction is less literal. Lace and eyelet are increasingly mixed with leather, tailored shorts, capris and sharper shoes rather than worn as a complete coquette uniform.

How do you wear lace casually?

Pair one lace piece with a plain, dry fabric such as denim, poplin, wool or leather. Keep jewellery minimal and choose a practical shoe. The contrast makes the lace read as texture rather than occasionwear.

What colours work with romantic clothing?

Cream and black remain useful, but cobalt, turquoise, mustard and dark red make romantic pieces feel current. Use one saturated colour near the face or at the shoe rather than matching every accessory.

The best romantic clothes contain a little argument

A good outfit does not need every piece to agree. The lace can remember ceremony while the Bermuda short insists on daytime. The leather jacket can make a cream dress less fragile. A red heel can add feeling without asking the rest of the look to become decorative.

That is why the return of romantic fashion feels useful rather than merely cyclical. It offers detail to wardrobes that have lived on disciplined basics, but it does not require abandoning those basics. Keep one thing tender and let another thing refuse it. The conversation between them is the style.
