Clerestory Windows Let the Sky In

A high strip of glass can change a room while leaving the walls below available for everyday life.

by.foro Editorial10 October 20267 min read

Clerestory windows are high windows set near the top of a wall, often above the usual line of sight. They bring daylight into a room while leaving the lower wall available for furniture, cabinetry or a more private boundary. Their effect depends on orientation, roof shape, neighbouring buildings and the way the light meets the ceiling.

You may notice the light before you notice the window. The upper wall looks softer, the ceiling seems less heavy, and a room with a fairly solid exterior gains a view of the sky. That is the particular appeal of clerestory glazing. It changes the atmosphere without requiring a whole wall of glass at eye level.

The short answer

Use high glazing where daylight matters more than a low view

Clerestory windows are worth considering beside a close boundary, above kitchen units or in a room where the walls below need to remain useful. Check the sun, overlooking, shading, operation and cleaning access as part of the design. High glass still needs a complete window specification.

What makes a window a clerestory?

The defining feature is its high position in the wall. A clerestory may be a continuous band, a series of smaller panes or glazing between different roof heights. It does not have to wrap the entire room or belong to a dramatic new house.

A rooflight sits within the roof plane. Clerestory glazing is generally vertical or near vertical, which gives it a different relationship to the sky, roof edge and walls. Both can introduce daylight, but their construction, shading and cleaning arrangements differ. Choose for the room and the site rather than treating the names as interchangeable.

A high internal window can look similar from one side, yet it borrows light from another room. An exterior clerestory has a direct route to daylight. Our internal window guide covers the borrowed-light version and the privacy questions it brings indoors.

How high windows change the daylight

When a window sits near the ceiling, light can reach upper surfaces that an ordinary low opening leaves shaded. A pale ceiling can help distribute that brightness, although the actual result depends on geometry and the light available outside. A narrow window looking into a tall neighbouring wall will behave differently from one looking at open sky.

Ask to see a section drawing. Roof height, eave depth and the position of the window head are difficult to judge from a floor plan. A clerestory can look generous in elevation while being tucked under an overhang that blocks much of its useful sky view.

Direct sun also needs attention. A high window can cast a beautiful moving patch across a wall, but that same patch may produce glare on a screen or heat the room at an inconvenient time. Consider the furniture layout and seasonal sun together. A daylight strategy should account for an ordinary working afternoon as well as the best morning photograph.

Are clerestory windows more private?

They can reduce direct views between a room and people outside at a similar ground level. A higher sill often lets a boundary-facing wall admit light without putting the sofa on display. That is useful on tight plots, where a conventional picture window might mostly frame a fence.

High does not automatically mean private. A neighbour upstairs, a sloping site or a raised terrace can change the sightline. Check the room from the relevant viewpoints, including after dark when indoor lights make the glazing more visible.

If privacy is the main reason for the window, draw those views before choosing the glass. Obscured glazing may suit some positions, but it changes the quality of the sky view and does not remove the need to consider placement. Privacy and daylight work best when they are designed together.

Where clerestory windows earn their place

Above a kitchen wall

A high band can bring daylight over a run of worktop or cabinets. Coordinate the sill with the joinery and leave space for the window frame, finishes and any opening mechanism. The drawing needs to show how the kitchen and glazing meet, not simply a bright strip above a generic cabinet block.

Along a private living-room edge

Keeping lower walls solid can provide room for art, books and a sofa while the upper glazing brings in sky. It is especially useful when the garden-facing side already has a generous opening. The high windows can balance the room's light rather than compete with the main view.

In a compact extension

A change in roof height can create space for a clerestory where the old and new buildings meet. Its usefulness depends on the surrounding roofs and orientation. Have the daylight assessed before committing to a complicated roof junction solely to obtain the window.

For another way to bring light into the centre of a plan, compare our small courtyard house ideas. The floor-space and construction implications are different, even when the aim is similar.

Fixed or opening glazing?

Fixed panes can offer a clean appearance and avoid the need for daily operation. They still need access for cleaning and maintenance. Opening units may support ventilation, but their benefit depends on a usable air path and the conditions outside.

If the window is out of reach, decide how it will open before ordering. Manual poles, controls and motors have different practical implications. Ask what happens during rain, whether operation is accessible to the people using the room and how a failed mechanism would be serviced.

Do the same for shading. A blind that cannot be reached easily is a poor answer to frequent glare. External shade, glass specification and the roof design should be considered together, with a solution appropriate to the climate and orientation.

A project to study for the idea

Bobo Architecture's Clerestory Laneway House uses staggered rooflines to bring northern light into a compact plan. The studio's project page describes the relationship between daylight, privacy and ceiling height, and lists the project as under construction at the time of review.

Study the roof section and the placement of the living spaces rather than treating the imagery as a universal recipe. Northern light has a specific meaning in the Vancouver setting. The equivalent orientation and shading choices in another climate may be different.

Clerestory window questions

Do clerestory windows have to open?

No. They can be fixed or opening. Choose according to the ventilation strategy, weather exposure, access and maintenance needs.

Will high windows make a room overheat?

They can contribute to overheating if solar gain is poorly managed. Orientation, glazing, shade and the rest of the building design determine the risk.

Can clerestory windows provide complete privacy?

Not in every setting. Check views from higher neighbouring windows and changes in ground level, as well as views from the street.
