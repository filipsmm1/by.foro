A Kitchen Rooflight Works Best Where the Daylight Is Missing

The brightest patch in the photograph is only part of the story. Start with the room that needs the light.

by.foro Editorial24 October 20267 min read

In this guide

  2. Find the part of the kitchen that needs daylight
  3. Three rooflight layouts worth considering

A kitchen-extension rooflight is most useful when it brings daylight to a part of the plan that the garden windows cannot reach. That might be the old rear room, a worktop beside a boundary wall or a dining table in the middle of the house. Choose its position alongside the kitchen layout, then plan the glass, shade, ventilation and cleaning access as one job.

Rear extensions have a familiar blind spot. The new room beside the garden looks lovely, while the room you walked through to get there has become a little gloomy. A bigger set of doors at the back does not always fix it. Sometimes the useful move is to put daylight further into the plan.

The short answer

Put the opening where it earns its place

Mark the darkest useful area on the floor plan before drawing a rooflight. Compare a narrow strip beside the kitchen, a compact opening over the table and glazing near the junction of old and new rooms. Check the roof structure and the sun's path, and include shading and maintenance in the brief.

Find the part of the kitchen that needs daylight

Walk through the existing house on an ordinary cloudy day. Where do you turn on a light first? Is the problem the sink, the route through the room or the old dining area that the extension will sit beyond? Those are different problems, and they may need different openings.

Draw the extension together with the rooms behind it. A rooflight positioned near the original rear wall can help the connection between old and new feel less abrupt, but a beam, deep ceiling or tall cupboard may interrupt the light. Ask the designer to show a section through this junction. A plan tells you where the glass sits; the section explains what lies between the glass and the room.

Also mark the places where strong light would be unwelcome. A screen used for work, a glossy counter or the chair facing the afternoon sun deserves attention before the opening is fixed. Good daylight should make the room easier to use.

Three rooflight layouts worth considering

A narrow strip beside the kitchen

A long opening can bring light along the depth of a narrow extension. Visually, it can separate a solid wall from the ceiling and make a modest room feel less hemmed in. Keep the relationship to cabinets deliberate. A strip that stops awkwardly above one cupboard can look like the kitchen and roof were designed on different days.

A compact rooflight above the table

This gives the dining area a clear centre without needing an elaborate ceiling. Check the table in its everyday position, including the space for chairs to move. If you regularly shift the furniture, a slightly less exact alignment may suit the room better than a composition that only works for one photograph.

Glazing near the old rear wall

This is worth exploring when the middle room is the weak point. The opening needs to work with the structure at the former external wall and the ceiling levels on either side. It is a design conversation to have early, while the extension's shape can still change.

The reveal matters as much as the rectangle of glass

A rooflight is seen through the thickness of the roof. The depth, angle and finish of that opening affect how it feels from below. A deep shaft can frame a beautiful patch of sky, but it can also conceal much of the glass when viewed from the adjoining room.

Ask to see the opening from the places where you will spend time: at the sink, seated at the table and entering from the hall. A perspective taken directly beneath the glass gives you very little information about those everyday views.

Keep ceiling fittings on the same drawing. Pendant lights, extraction, downlights and blind housings all need space. If the rooflight is above the dining table, the pendant does not automatically belong directly under it. A wall light or a pendant moved to another part of the room may leave the sky view clearer.

Choose shade before the first hot afternoon

Daylight and summer comfort need to be considered together. The orientation, amount of glass, surrounding shade and the rest of the extension all affect the result. Ask the designer or glazing supplier to explain the proposed solar-control specification in the context of your room, rather than choosing glass from a single headline figure.

Check which shading products fit the exact rooflight, how they are operated and where the controls will go. The VELUX flat-glass rooflight product sheet, for example, describes compatible anti-heat and blackout options. That is a reminder to specify the complete system; it is not a claim that every blind fits every opening.

An opening rooflight may form part of a ventilation strategy, but it does not replace a properly planned kitchen extraction system. Think about rain, security and what happens when nobody is home to operate it. If it is powered, arrange the wiring and controls before the ceiling is finished.

Leave enough solid ceiling for the room to settle

There is no rule that a successful extension needs the largest possible glazed roof. Solid areas provide places for insulation, services and lighting, and can give the room a more sheltered atmosphere. A modest opening in the right position can be a more satisfying feature than glass spread across the entire ceiling.

Relate the rooflight to the room's other openings. A large garden door already makes a strong visual statement. The roof glazing might work better as a quieter source of light further back. Keep the material palette simple enough for the moving daylight to be noticed.

If the roof cannot reasonably be altered, compare high wall glazing where the site allows it. Our clerestory window guide looks at daylight above eye level. For a hall beside a bright room, an internal window is another conversation, with a different scope of work.

Ask how it will be installed, cleaned and repaired

Before ordering, have the roof structure, waterproofing, insulation and surrounding construction designed for the chosen unit. Installation requirements vary. A dimension or roof pitch from one product sheet should not be transferred to a different rooflight because the photographs look similar.

Cleaning is easy to forget when the view is all sky. Establish how both sides of the glazing can be reached safely, who will do the work and whether the outside needs specialist access. In the VELUX sheet linked above, exterior access is required to clean the top unit. Check your proposed product just as carefully.

Ask for the full scope in the quotation: the unit, installation, weathering details, interior finishing, controls and any shading. A low price for the glass itself tells you little about the completed room. Keep the specification and maintenance information somewhere you can find them after the builders leave.

Kitchen rooflight questions

Where should a rooflight go in a kitchen extension?

Start with the area that lacks useful daylight, then coordinate the opening with the structure, worktops, table and sun exposure. The centre of the roof is not automatically the best position.

Is a rooflight over a dining table a good idea?

It can give the table a pleasing focus. Check glare, summer shade, evening lighting and whether the furniture is likely to move.

Do kitchen rooflights need blinds?

That depends on the glazing, orientation and room. Consider compatible shading during design so that a later fitting does not become an awkward compromise.
